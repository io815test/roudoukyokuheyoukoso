# Technical Plausibility Check

Use this skill to keep speculative technology story-useful and internally coherent.

## Read First

Read only relevant sources:

- `canon/world-setting.md`
- technology or organization files linked from it
- `drafts/reference/第二部_適正処理済_企画メモ.md` for Part 2 environmental and mining material
- current draft scene if checking prose

## Evaluation Axes

Check the idea across four axes:

1. **Mechanism**: what physically or procedurally makes it work.
2. **Constraint**: what it cannot do, what breaks, what costs time or resources.
3. **Operational use**: who can use it under stress, with what training, and what failure mode.
4. **Story effect**: whether it creates choices instead of solving the scene for free.

## Project-Specific Hotspots

- LM-series equipment and nonlethal weapons must preserve escalation discipline.
- Electromagnetic weapons still need feeding, power, heat, safety interlocks, and failure recovery.
- VTOL and military support should respect airspace, danger-close, logistics, and command authority.
- Environmental analysis should distinguish direct evidence from inference: fish tissue, gill residue, water samples, satellite imagery, current models, and soil samples do not prove the same thing.
- Industrial waste schemes should have paperwork, contractors, sampling fraud, transport routes, and subsidy incentives.
- Near-future details should feel operational rather than magical.

## Output Contract

Give:

- verdict: works / works with caveats / weak
- what supports plausibility
- what needs modification
- prose-ready replacement lines if useful

Do not over-explain real science unless the user asks. Keep enough detail to make the scene defensible.
