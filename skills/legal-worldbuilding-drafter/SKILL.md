## Output Shapes

Choose the smallest useful form.

- **Scene hook**: one or two paragraphs explaining what authority lets a character act.
- **Setting note**: short canon-ready section with purpose, scope, limits, and abuses.
- **Statute draft**: article-style clauses only when the user asks for law text.
- **Operational checklist**: required approvals, evidence, agency handoff, and failure risks.

## Drafting Rules

- Keep laws functional. Every statute should answer: who can act, against whom, when, with what limits, and what happens if abused.
- Prefer cross-law overlap when realistic. Labor, police, environmental, transport, and machine laws may all touch one incident.
- Preserve the story's institutional tension: 労働局 is powerful but not omnipotent.
- Separate **authority to investigate**, **authority to seize**, **authority to arrest**, **authority to use force**, and **authority to share data**.
- Avoid perfect laws. Leave political compromise, loopholes, exception clauses, and agency friction.
- For penalties, keep proportionality visible: administrative sanction, fine, imprisonment, licensing revocation, corporate debarment, and criminal referral are different tools.

## Story Fit Checks

Before finalizing, check:

- Does this make the current scene easier to understand without draining tension?
- Does this preserve the need for other agencies such as 国家警察, SAG, CTRU, 水上臨検隊, or 資源環境監督庁?
- Does the law create a usable constraint, not just permission?
- Would a hostile corporation or captured bureaucrat have a way to exploit the wording?

## Response Contract

When answering, provide:

1. recommended legal frame
2. short in-world wording or statute draft
3. limits and abuse risks
4. how to use it in prose
